## Part 2 — Guideline 4.3(b): **do not answer this in Resolution Center**

⚠️ **Superseded 2026-08-06 by the full rejection text.** Apple's actual wording:

> "These app features may be useful, informative or entertaining, and the app
> **may include features or characteristics that distinguish it. However, there
> are already enough of these apps on the App Store.** … We encourage you to
> **reconsider the app concept and submit a new app** …"

That middle sentence **pre-rejects the differentiation argument**. A reply that
lists what makes Baybe different is answering a question Apple explicitly
foreclosed, and it will read as not having understood the rejection. The
earlier six-point draft has been deleted for that reason.

The only instrument that can move a concept-level 4.3(b) is an **App Review
Board appeal**, and the argument has to be *structural* — that Baybe is not the
kind of app they are comparing it to — not a feature list.

### Appeal draft (App Review Board, not Resolution Center)

> We are appealing the 4.3(b) rejection of Baybe (app 6793496424, submission
> 9942a9c1-3993-44dc-8e2e-7f48dcccdc5b).
>
> We accept that the dating category is saturated and that a list of
> differentiating features is not a counter-argument. We are not making that
> argument. We are asking the Board to consider whether Baybe belongs to the
> category it was compared against at all.
>
> The apps 4.3(b) is protecting the Store from share one core loop: browse
> profiles, express interest, exchange text messages. Every feature in those
> apps is arranged around that loop.
>
> **Baybe makes that loop impossible.** There is no profile deck to browse —
> none exists anywhere in the app. There is no way to send a message to another
> user, ever, under any circumstance, until the two people have completed a
> live call together and then each *independently and privately* opted in
> afterward. A user cannot accumulate matches, cannot maintain an inbox, and
> cannot initiate contact in text.
>
> The unit of interaction is a synchronous voice or video call that our server
> terminates at 180 seconds. The media credential expires with the call clock,
> so no client can extend one. The constraint is not a feature layered on top
> of a messaging app; it is the substrate.
>
> The clearest way to see this: **remove live calling from Baybe and there is
> no product left** — no browsing, no messaging, nothing a user could do.
> Remove live calling from a conventional dating app and it is essentially
> unchanged. We believe that is the test for whether something duplicates an
> existing category or constitutes a different one.
>
> We are aware of apps that add video calling to a conventional dating core.
> Baybe has no conventional core to add it to. If the Board's view is that this
> distinction is insufficient, we would be grateful to know that plainly, so we
> can decide whether to rebuild the concept or take it off iOS — rather than
> resubmitting into the same outcome.

**Honest note before sending:** "video speed dating" is itself a genre with
prior art (Filter Off, Blindlee, and others). The argument above is the
strongest honest one available, and it may still fail. See
`RESUBMISSION_CHECKLIST_2026-08-06.md` Gate 7 for the realistic odds.
